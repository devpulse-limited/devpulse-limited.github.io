Privacy Policy

Last updated 7 November 2026

The short version

Coconut Rocket is free. After a crash you can choose to watch an ad to keep flying. That ad comes from Google AdMob, which collects some device and usage data to show and measure ads. You choose whether ads can be personalised, and whether apps may track you, and the game plays exactly the same either way.

We, DevPulse, do not run servers for Coconut Rocket and do not receive personal data about you. Your progress and settings stay on your phone. No account, no analytics, no purchases.

Who we are

Coconut Rocket is made by DevPulse, the trading name of Devpulse Limited, based in the United Kingdom, at devpulse.co.uk. In this policy, we and us mean DevPulse. For the data that the optional ad involves, Google processes it as described below and in its own policies.

What stays on your phone

The game keeps a save file on your device only. It holds

  • your coconuts, the characters and rockets you own and the ones you fly,
  • your missions, your daily streak, your best scores and how many runs you made each day,
  • your settings for controls, sound, music, volume and vibration.

If the game ever crashes, a short technical report is kept on your phone too. None of this is ever sent to us. Deleting the app deletes it. A backup of your device, for example to iCloud, may include it, under the terms of Apple.

The daily shaft is worked out on your phone from the date, so the game itself needs no internet connection.

Device features

  • Motion sensors. If you choose tilt controls, the game reads the motion sensors of your phone to steer. These readings are not stored or sent anywhere.
  • Vibration. The game can vibrate your phone. You can turn this off in Settings.
  • Sharing. The Share score button opens the share sheet of your phone with a picture of your score card. Nothing is sent unless you choose where to share it.

The reward ad, from Google AdMob

When a run ends, the score card can offer an extra life in return for watching a short video ad. Watching is always your choice, and ads never appear during a run. The ads come from Google AdMob through the Google Mobile Ads SDK. So that an ad is ready when you want one, the game asks Google for an ad in the background soon after it opens, which means the Google software runs while you play, even if you never watch an ad.

To choose, show and measure ads, and to prevent fraud, the Google software in the app collects and shares

  • device identifiers, which means the advertising identifier of Apple, called the IDFA, only if you allow tracking as described below, plus identifiers limited to the app or to us,
  • approximate location, estimated from your IP address and never from GPS,
  • advertising and usage data, such as which ads were shown, video views, taps on ads and app launches,
  • diagnostics, which means crash and performance data of the ad software.

Google may share this data with its advertising partners, which the consent form lists. Google acts as an independent controller of this data. To see how Google uses it, read How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services, and the Google privacy policy at policies.google.com/privacy.

Coconut Rocket asks Google only for ads suitable for a general audience with parental guidance, rated PG.

Your choices for consent and tracking

  • Consent in the UK, the EEA and Switzerland. Before any ad is requested, the consent form from Google, called the User Messaging Platform, asks whether Google and its partners may use your data for personalised ads and the other purposes listed there. You can change your answer at any time in Coconut Rocket. Tap the gear on the home screen, then Privacy choices.
  • App Tracking Transparency on iPhone. iOS then asks whether Coconut Rocket may track you across apps and websites of other companies. If you choose Ask App Not to Track, the advertising identifier is not shared, and any ads you watch are less personal. You can change this later in the Settings app of your iPhone, under Privacy and Security, then Tracking.
  • Saying no to either question never changes the game itself. Every feature, character and rocket works the same.
  • You can also reset your advertising identifier, or turn off personalised ads from Apple, in the Settings app under Privacy and Security, then Apple Advertising.

What we do not do

  • We do not collect, store on our servers or sell personal data, and there is no analytics or crash reporting software of our own in the game.
  • There are no accounts and no in app purchases. Coconuts are earned by playing.
  • Coconut Rocket does not ask for your camera, microphone, photos, contacts or precise location.

App Store

Apple handles downloads and updates and may collect data about that under its own policies. We see the anonymous, aggregated statistics that Apple shows every developer, for example downloads per country, which do not identify you.

Legal bases under UK GDPR and GDPR

  • For personalised ads and the advertising identifier, the basis is your consent, given through the consent form and the tracking prompt. You can withdraw it at any time as described above.
  • For non personalised ads, ad measurement and fraud prevention, the basis is our legitimate interest in funding a free game, and that of Google, as described in the Google policies.

Children

Coconut Rocket is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal data from children. Ads in the game are limited to content rated PG.

How long data is kept

The save file stays on your phone until you delete the app. Google keeps the data described above for the periods set out in its policies.

Changes to this policy

If a future version of Coconut Rocket changes what is collected, we will update this page and its date before that version is released, and the privacy details in the App Store will change with it.

Contact

For questions about privacy in Coconut Rocket, email support@devpulse.co.uk.
