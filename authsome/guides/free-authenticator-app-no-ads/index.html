Which Authenticator Apps Are Free With No Ads or Subscription?

2FAS and Proton Authenticator say in writing that they have no ads, and both are free on the App Store with no In App Purchases listed. Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator and Ente Auth are free too, and none of the five lists any data used to track you on its privacy label. Making 2FA codes needs no server, so no app needs a subscription to make them.

An authenticator app makes the codes for two factor authentication, or 2FA. These are the facts on the UK App Store page of each app, read on 19 November 2026. The list is in no order and is not a ranking.

The last column comes from the privacy label on each page, under the heading Data Used to Track You. The developer fills the label in, and the App Store says Apple has not verified it. Apple counts as tracking the linking of data about you with data from other companies for targeted advertising, and sharing it with a data broker. So the column tells you about your data. It is not a promise about adverts.

Two makers do make that promise. 2FAS states that its apps have no ads or tracking, on the page where it asks for donations. Proton states that Proton Authenticator has no ads and no tracking. The pages read for Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator and Ente Auth do not mention adverts either way.

The Passwords app from Apple also makes 2FA codes. It is part of iOS 18 and macOS Sequoia and later, so there is nothing to buy or install. How to AutoFill 2FA codes on iPhone covers it.

Why Should an Authenticator App Not Need a Subscription?

An authenticator app makes each 2FA code from a secret key, which the service gives it inside a QR code when you turn on 2FA. Apple calls it a setup key, and AuthSome calls it a Secret. From then on the app works out each 2FA code from two things, that secret key and the time.

The calculation runs on your phone. Google says its own app still makes 2FA codes with no internet connection or mobile service. The maker of the app does not have to run a server each time you sign in, so a fee is not covering the cost of making your 2FA codes.

Some makers charge for extras. But if an app asks for a weekly payment before it will show a 2FA code at all, you are not paying for anything a free app cannot do.

How Do You Spot an App That Charges Weekly Before You Install It?

Any developer can publish an app with the word Authenticator in its name, so the name and the icon tell you little. Two lines on the App Store page tell you more, and you can read both before you tap Get.

  1. Open the page of the app in the App Store.
  2. Find the developer name on the page. For Google Authenticator it is Google. For Microsoft Authenticator it is Microsoft Corporation. If you meant to install one of those and the name is a different company, you have the wrong app.
  3. Look near the price or the Get button for the label In App Purchases. Apple says this label marks an app that sells something inside it, and that subscriptions are one kind of In App Purchase.
  4. If the label is there, look through the rest of the page for what is sold and for how much. A price per week is a subscription.

Apple says subscriptions can renew weekly, monthly, every three months or yearly, and that they renew by themselves until you cancel. A free trial does not change that. Apple advises cancelling a trial at least 24 hours before it ends if you do not want to pay.

The label is not proof of a bad app, since many honest apps sell extras. It is your cue to read what is sold before you install.

How Do You Cancel a Subscription You Already Started?

Do not delete the app yet. This guide calls the line in an authenticator app for one service an entry. Makers call it an account, a token or a code, and AuthSome calls it an Account. Your entries may exist only inside that app, and deleting it could lock you out of every service it holds.

Cancelling stops the next payment. Apple does not say whether an app keeps working after you cancel, and what it shows once the paid time ends is up to the app. So if no payment is due in the next day or two, move your entries first, using the steps in the next section, then cancel. If a payment or the end of a trial is close, cancel now and move your entries straight after.

Apple gives these steps for iPhone and iPad.

  1. Open Settings and tap your name.
  2. Tap Subscriptions.
  3. Tap the subscription.
  4. Tap Cancel Subscription. You may need to scroll down to find it.

If there is no Cancel button, or you see an expiry message in red, Apple says the subscription is already cancelled. If the subscription is not in the list, search your email for a receipt from Apple and check which Apple Account it names. It may be a different Apple Account of yours, or one that belongs to a family member, who then has to cancel it.

How Do You Move Away Safely?

Move while the old app still shows your 2FA codes, and keep it until the last step.

  1. Install the app you are moving to, and keep the old app on your phone.
  2. Look in the old app for an export or transfer option. If it has one, check that your new app can read it. Google Authenticator, for example, has its own export, covered in how to export Google Authenticator to another app.
  3. If there is no export, move one service at a time. Sign in to each service, open its security settings, and set up the authenticator app again so that it shows a new QR code. Scan that with the new app and type in the 2FA code it shows.
  4. Sign out of each service and back in with a 2FA code from the new app.
  5. Delete the old app only when every service works in the new one.

While you are in each service, save the recovery codes it offers. They are your way back in if a phone is ever lost.

Common Questions

Is There an Authenticator App Without Ads?

Yes. 2FAS and Proton both state that their authenticator apps have no ads, and AuthSome, which is coming soon, has none. The pages read for Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator and Ente Auth do not mention adverts either way, and none of the five lists data used to track you on its App Store privacy label.

Is There an Authenticator App You Can Use Without a Login?

Yes. Google has a help section on using Google Authenticator without a Google Account, and 2FAS says on its App Store page that no account is required. AuthSome has nothing to sign up for.

Why Does My Authenticator App Charge Weekly?

Because it sells a subscription as an In App Purchase, and Apple lets subscriptions renew as often as weekly. You can see it under Subscriptions in Settings, after you tap your name, and cancel it there.

Are Free Authenticator Apps Safe to Use?

Price does not tell you. Any authenticator app works out the same 2FA codes from the same secret key, so look at who the developer is, how the app backs up what you add to it and whether you can export it.
