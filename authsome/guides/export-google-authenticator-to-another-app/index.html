How to Export Google Authenticator to Another App on iPhone

Google Authenticator can show your accounts as an export QR code, and other authenticator apps on iPhone can scan it. Scan it from inside the new app, not with the Camera app. Keep Google Authenticator until every two factor authentication code works in the new app.

By Junaid Younus. Published 19 November 2026.

Make the Export QR Code in Google Authenticator

Google Authenticator makes the short numbers that services ask for after your password, known as two factor authentication or 2FA codes. These export steps are from the Google help page for iPhone and iPad. Google says you need the latest version of Google Authenticator on the phone that holds your accounts.

  1. Open Google Authenticator and tap Menu.
  2. Choose Transfer accounts, then Export accounts.
  3. Prove it is you when the app asks, the same way you open your phone.
  4. Select the accounts you want to move.
  5. Tap Next. The QR code appears.

Google says that if you move more than one account, the app may make more than one QR code. Its help page gives no number of accounts for each one. The new app needs every QR code in the set, so scan them all.

Treat the export QR code like a password. Anyone who scans it gets the secret keys that make your 2FA codes.

Which Apps Can Scan the Export QR Code?

Google wrote the export for moving to Google Authenticator on another device, and its help page names no other app. Other apps read the same QR code, and these say so in their own help pages.

  • 2FAS. Open the menu, choose Import tokens, then Google Authenticator, then Scan QR code.
  • Ente Auth. Open Settings, choose Data, then Import codes, then Google Authenticator. Scan each QR code of the export in turn. Ente says the accounts are added after the last one.
  • AuthSome. Coming soon to the App Store for iOS 26 or later. Tap +, then Import Accounts, then choose Google Authenticator and scan each QR code it shows. You choose the Profile, and you see everything that was found before anything is added.

Apple Passwords also makes 2FA codes, but the Apple guide describes setting them up one website at a time and does not mention this export. If your app is not listed here, search its help pages for Google Authenticator before you start.

Why Not Scan It With the iPhone Camera?

The export QR code is not a web address and it is not the usual QR code a website shows when you turn on 2FA. The Google help page does not say what is inside it. The source code of Aegis, an open source authenticator app for Android, reads it as a link that begins otpauth-migration:// with the accounts packed inside. The QR code a website shows when you turn on 2FA holds a different link, one that begins otpauth:// and carries a single account.

Apple says the Camera app shows a link to tap when it sees a QR code, for things such as websites, apps and tickets. Neither Apple nor Google says the Camera app can open an export from Google Authenticator, so do not expect it to. Open the new authenticator app first and use the scanner inside its import screen.

What If Both Apps Are on the Same iPhone?

A phone cannot point its camera at its own screen. You have two choices.

  • Take a screenshot of each export QR code, then give the screenshots to the new app. AuthSome does this from the same import step, where you choose each screenshot from Photos. 2FAS says its iPhone app can do the same, with Choose QR code on its Import tokens screen. With another app, check that its import screen can read a QR code from an image.
  • Install the new app on a second device, such as an iPad, and scan the iPhone screen with it. This suits an app that keeps its accounts in step across your devices. Using the same authenticator app on two devices explains which apps do that.

A screenshot of an export QR code is as sensitive as the QR code itself. Delete each one as soon as the import is done, and do not leave them where your photos are shared or backed up.

Does Exporting Remove Anything From Google Authenticator?

The Google help page does not describe the export removing anything. Its export steps end with a confirmation on the new device, and it describes deleting as a separate action that you carry out one account at a time and confirm. Google does not say in so many words that the accounts stay, so check for yourself. Go back to the main list after the export and make sure every account is still there.

If Google Authenticator ever offers to remove the accounts you exported, keep them. Nothing is lost by having the same account in two apps for a few days. Both apps make the same 2FA codes, because they hold the same secret key.

What to Check Before You Delete Anything

  1. Count the accounts. The new app should show as many as you selected in Google Authenticator. If some are missing, you may have skipped one QR code of the set.
  2. Compare one 2FA code. Open the same account in both apps at the same moment. The two numbers should match.
  3. Sign in somewhere with the new app. Start with the services you could least afford to lose, such as your email.
  4. Check the names. Make sure each account shows the right service and the right username, and correct any that came across blank.
  5. Check that the new app is backed up, so the move does not leave you with a single copy.
  6. Delete any screenshots of the export QR codes.

Only then remove accounts from Google Authenticator. Google says that if your 2FA codes are saved to your Google Account, deleting one deletes it from every device where they are synced.

If you are moving because of a new phone, read whether authenticator apps transfer to a new iPhone before you wipe the old one. If the accounts are on an Android phone, see how to move 2FA codes from Android to iPhone.

Common Questions

Can You Export a Single Account From Google Authenticator?

It should. Google says to select the accounts you want to transfer and sets no minimum, so select only the one you need.

Can You Export the Secret Key From Google Authenticator?

The Google help page describes no way to show the secret key of an account as text. The export QR code is the only route it documents. If you need the key itself, ask the service for a new setup key, which means turning 2FA off and on again there. Have a recovery code ready before you do.

Why Is Google Authenticator Export Not Working?

Google says the phone you export from needs the latest version of Google Authenticator. If the export made more than one QR code, the new app needs all of them. If the new app still refuses, export a few accounts at a time and import each group.

What Is an otpauth-migration:// Link?

It is the start of the link inside the export QR code from Google Authenticator. The Google help page does not mention it. The source code of Aegis, an open source authenticator app, reads it.
